# Wacom Cintiq Pro 23 touch (DTH-3220) notes

## Overview

The Cintiq Pro 32 Touch was released around 2018. It still is an amazing drawing device, given its age and size. It is actually as good as anything else on the market in 2026. It has an excellent drawing experience driven by the included Pro Pen 2, model number KP504E, and an absolutely gigantic screen.

## Should you get the tablet in 2026?

This is a nuanced issue. On the one hand, the tablet's drawing performance and quality of display are excellent. I don't have any hesitation in saying that it will work as well as any other tablet that is available. On the other hand, because it is approaching 10 years old, we do expect Wacom to drop support for this tablet in their tablet drivers at some point. That could happen anytime from this year, 2026, to the next six years, I would estimate. So if you do choose to get this tablet, you should be cognizant that when the driver support stops, you're going to have to deal with that somehow. You could use an older driver, or you could use open tablet driver. At some point, an older driver will eventually not work on a more modern version of Windows or Mac OS. We just don't know when that might occur. In terms of price, it varies quite a bit on sites like eBay. I have seen it listed from $3,000 to $1,200. I myself bought a unit on eBay that cost about $1,300 once you include tax. As with any used drawing tablet, you should investigate the kinds of issues that occur with used tablets and the status of the tablet with the seller.

## Included Pen

The tablet comes with the Wacom Pro Pen, model number KP504E. As of 2026, this pen is still likely the best drawing pen that has ever existed, with a very low initial activation force, a very high maximum pressure, and good ergonomics.

## Ergonomics

### Dealing with the size

This tablet is the largest Pen display ever made. If you decide to use this tablet, you should be aware that you will essentially have to reserve a desk for using it. It will occupy so much space on your desk that you will be unable to do anything else. Because it is very heavy, it is also incredibly difficult to move. So, you aren't going to be able to push it out of the way easily. Because it occupies so much space, it will make it challenging to reach for a keyboard if you use a lot of keyboard shortcuts. So, consider looking into auxiliary input devices like the TourBox.

I also found a tendency for my drawing tablet pens to roll under the tablet, which requires me to lift up the tablet from one side to see if anything's hidden underneath.

Because the tablet is so big, some people, especially if they're smaller, may have trouble reaching the top edge of the tablet screen or the top corners. For this reason, if you're thinking about getting this device, I recommend creating a cardboard mockup that includes its bezel and drawing a rectangle where the screen is. This way, you can simulate what it would feel like for you. You want to avoid a situation where you get this amazing tablet, but you're having to move your arm around so much that it gets very tired.

### Stands

This tablet does not come with a stand.

### Legs

This tablet does come with two folding legs. I found that the legs put the tablet at enough of an angle that it's very comfortable to use, though some people may want to get a stand to achieve a higher angle.

## Connections and Cabling

### Ports

Power - This tablet has a port for power and then it also has three separate ways to get a video signal.

Video - There are three separate ways of getting a video signal to the  tablet: DisplayPort, HDMI, and USB-C.

## Drawing experience

### Overview

The drawing experience is excellent, there is no doubt about that. Across the board, Wacom still continues to be the best drawing experience ever provided in this space.

### Diagonal wobble

RATING: VERY GOOD (VERY LOW)

Diagonal wobble was very good. There were only very tiny amounts of wobble present in the strokes that I observed at different speeds. My feeling is the amount of wobble I saw is better than most Pen displays. In fact, I think it's a little bit better even than the Cintiq Pro 27.

### Lag

The lag is typical for a Pen display of this size. As you draw, you can see the pointer trailing the physical tip of the pen a little bit. There was nothing especially unusual about the amount of lag; it was very typical for what we see inn pen displays.
