# Inspiroy Dial 2 V2 (Q640M) notes

### Overview

* First Huion **pen tablet** with **PenTech 4.0**, shipping with the **PW600** pen.
* The PW600 pen is so good at pressure, has three buttons and an eraser — this makes the Dial 2 V2 the **best non-Wacom pen tablet on the market as of September 2026**, and very close to what you'd expect from an Intuos Pro. Many people would not be able to tell the difference. **A landmark for non-Wacom brands.** I've never felt any of them came this close before - again due to the excellent PW600 pen.
* To be clear: **Wacom's core engineering is definitely still ahead** — I know enough about the Intuos Pro technology to say that. But for the vast majority of people the difference would be undetectable, and based on my testing I couldn't tell the difference either.
* **An easy recommendation.**
* Because I'm so satisfied with the performance of this tablet with the new PW600 pen, I'm looking forward to Huion releasing additional upgrades to their other tablets that let them use the PW600 pen.

## Pens

### Included Pen: PW600

* **Unit-to-unit consistency: excellent.** The bundled PW600 matched two other PW600 pens I own on both IAF and maximum pressure. Typical for the PW600 series — very good consistency.
* **Slight hyperreactivity at low IAF.** Typical of EMR pens; all EMR pens have this.
* **Recommendation for normal use:** a slightly harder pressure curve + pressure smoothing in the app. Much better stroke quality at low pressure near the IAF.

### Pen cross-compatibility

* Tried the tablet with other PW600 pens that shipped with other tablets — **all worked totally fine**, including "P2" PW600s.
* **PW600S** also tested — works totally fine.
* **Eraser end tried on all the pens** — worked as expected, with pressure sensitivity.

### Per-pen recognition

* Tried three pens: **PW600, PW600S, PW600L.**
* **The tablet knows the difference between all three.** Using one brings up a **distinct settings entry for that pen**, with its own **pressure curve** and other settings.
* Why it matters: per-pen pressure curves mean you can keep a harder curve on one pen and a softer one on another without reconfiguring — directly useful given my "use a slightly harder curve" recommendation. Also a genuine feature-level differentiator, not just a spec bump.

## Device

* **Physically identical to the previous Inspiroy Dial 2.** Only tell that it's the V2 is the model name on the back of the tablet.
* Design is very nice - good looking tablet

## Drawing experience

### Pressure scan rate test (many small dots)

* **All dots drawn. No strokes lost. Excellent.**

### Pressure control

* Testing: Repeatedly ramped pressure across the range — thin to thick and back, light to heavy — many times over. Strokes easy to control.&#x20;
* The wide _physical_ pressure range of the pen helps a lot.

### Surface texture

* Very slightly more textured than the Wacom Intuos Pro 2025 tablets; very close to the Intuos Pro 2017 tablets.
* Pleasant to draw on. Pen does not slip or slide on the surface.

### Pointer lag

* Pen felt **very responsive**. Subjectively very close to the **Wacom Intuos Pro** models.
* **A little less lag than the XPPen Deco Pro** models — consistent with my previous experience of those tablets.
* Overall pointer lag rating: **excellent (very low).**

### Diagonal wobble — rated excellent

* Couldn't really see any diagonal wobble. What's there is **microscopic, on par with the Wacom Intuos Pro models.**
* Same very low, almost imperceptible amount at **slow, medium and fast** speeds alike.

### Tilt compensation

* **Method:** hold the pen completely vertical and keep it stable, then tilt to \~50 degrees east, south, west and north.
* **Result:** minor pointer displacement/drift. Totally acceptable for drawing — I don't think anyone would notice it even on a pen display, and certainly not on a pen tablet.
* **Verdict:** not quite as good as a Wacom Intuos Pro (excellent tilt compensation on every one I've tried), but very close.

### Other inputs

* **No touch support.**
* **6 buttons + 2 dials.** Dials spin both directions and can be pressed. (Huion's spec sheet says "6 press keys, 2 dials, 2 function-switch buttons" — the 2 function-switch buttons look to be the dial presses.)
* Usage note: I'm right-handed: pen in right hand, left hand on the buttons and dials.
* I kept **accidentally pressing the lower dial**, so I often **disable the lower dial and use only the upper dial.**&#x20;

## Ergonomics

* **No replaceable surface**, unlike Intuos Pro 2017 and Intuos Pro 2025.

## Cabling & connectivity

### Bluetooth

* Connected successfully to a **Mac Studio**, drew in **Krita**. Drawing worked totally fine.
* **Slight pointer lag over Bluetooth** — definitely noticeable to me, though not everyone would necessarily detect it.
* **I don't generally recommend Bluetooth**: computers sometimes have flaky connections to Bluetooth devices, and it adds an extra point of lag.
* Possible corroboration: Huion's spec sheet lists the report rate as >300 PPS wired but **>133 PPS over Bluetooth** — though I flagged that figure as needing verification.
