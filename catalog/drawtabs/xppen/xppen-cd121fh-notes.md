# XP-Pen Artist 12 3rd (CD121FH) notes

## Overview

Good pen display at an excellent price. Will be good for beginners or for a first tablet.

Very convenient that the tablet comes with all the cables you need to connect via HDMI and USB-C (assuming your computer supports USB-C)

12" is the smallest I would recommend for a pen tablet. I personally prefer larger sizes (19" to 22") but for many this size should work.&#x20;

### Verdict

* I have no hesitation recommending this tablet. For the price, and given what it provides - especially the included full-featured USB-C cable - it's a lot of value. I think it's going to be a popular tablet.
* It's one of the least expensive pen displays you can get. At the time of my stream the normal price on the XP-Pen site was $240 and it was on sale for $216.
* The only negative I found is a tiny bit of diagonal wobble. Most people won't notice it, and if you do, a little smoothing makes it go away. See [Diagonal wobble](#diagonal-wobble).
* If it's your first pen display and you're comfortable working at this size, I think it's a great beginner tablet.
* Compared to the older [XP-Pen Artist 12 GEN2 (CD120FH)](xppen-cd120fh-notes.md), this one is easily better. You should get this one over the GEN2.
* The [Huion Kamvas 13 GEN3 (GS1333)](../huion/huion-gs1333-notes.md) might be a better tablet, but this one costs about $50 to $70 less, so I think a lot of people will get it for that reason. Some people may also prefer the way these buttons work. And the Kamvas 13 GEN3 only includes the 3-in-1 cable, not a full-featured USB-C cable.

## Notes on specs

### Device specs

* Both USB-C ports are recessed
* Next to the ports there are three buttons: a power button and plus/minus buttons for brightness.
* No legs and no VESA mount. The back is flat. If you want to put it on an arm, you'll need one designed for a small laptop or an iPad that holds it with brackets.
* No touch.
* Not wireless.

### Pens

* Included pen: see [XP-Pen X4 pen notes](../../pens/xppen-pens/xppen-x4pen-notes.md)
* XP-Pen does not list other pens as compatible - however these pens work
  * X3 Pro
  * X3 Pro Slim
  * X3 roller works (but roller cannot be configured)

## Design

* I like the design. It's all black plastic and it looks professional - like professional equipment. The older XP-Pen Artist 13 GEN2 and Artist 12 GEN2 have almost a deliberately consumer look to them.
* It reminds me of the Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon I used at Microsoft: it looks sturdy, like a real workhorse. I mean that as a compliment.
* XP-Pen does its own thing with design. It doesn't look like any other tablet on the market, with the rollers and the way the buttons stick out from the tablet. Nobody else is doing wide bezels like XP-Pen, and I give them credit for being brave about it.
* The bezel is about 3/4 of an inch.
* Size: physically it is very close in width and height to the [Wacom Movink 13 (DTH-135)](../wacom/wacom-movink/wacom-dth135-notes.md) (13.3"). But it is quite a bit thicker - I'd say about two and a half times thicker - and it's flat, where the Movink 13 is wedge shaped. Yes, it could be thinner, but I like it.
* It feels good and it's not heavy.
* The box is cute: colorful and bright, with two cute characters (a bunny and a fox) on it. A little different from XP-Pen's usual intricate box designs.

## Drawing experience

### Static tracking accuracy

GOOD.

### Corner and edge accuracy

TYPICAL - Slight deviation (\~2mm) at corners and edges

### Tilt compensation

Overall GOOD

But pointer deviates slightly to the left (\~1mm) as the pen tilts to the left.

### Diagonal wobble

* Slightly diagonal wobble visible in strokes at all tested speeds. With the most at the slowest speed and declining as the stroke speed increased.
* Most users will likely not notice any wobble.
* If it does show up smoothing will remove it. In Krita, weighted stabilization with a distance value of 200 removed it.
* How I tested: in Krita with the Ink 3 fine liner (no pressure on size, no smoothing), drawing 45° strokes against a straightedge at three speeds: slow (about 4 seconds per 10 cm), medium (about 2 seconds per 10 cm) and fast (about 1 second per 10 cm).
* With weighted smoothing at 50 (which is very low) the wobble was almost all gone. At 100 it was very, very minimal. At 200 you really can't see it.
* Remember this is a harsh test. It draws out wobble in situations most people would never notice. Lots of tablets, even Wacom tablets, have diagonal wobble that can be detected.
* It's on par with the other XP-Pen tablets I've used. In general I've seen a bit more diagonal wobble with XP-Pen than with Huion or Wacom.

### Artifacts at low pressure

MINIMAL (GOOD)

* How I tested: a large brush (about 300 px) with a flat pressure curve in Krita and a flat curve in the driver, drawing right above the IAF.
* The thin strokes were clean. No blobbiness, and no "pearls".
* This means you won't have to tweak your pressure curve to work around low-pressure artifacts. I wish everyone did it the way XP-Pen does it.

### Pressure banding

GOOD - no banding observed

* How I tested: low-pressure strokes with pressure mapped to opacity (not size), then auto-contrast to bring out the variation. No regular banding, no regular attenuation and no regular spikes.

### Pressure scan rate

GOOD. No missed strokes

### Parallax

Good (LOW)

### Pointer lag

Slightly more than I would expect for a pen display.

* A little more than I'd expect for a 13-inch pen display. Pointer lag seems to go down as displays get smaller.
* But it's typical for a pen display, and I don't think you'll notice it in normal drawing.

### Surface texture

Tested with the default plastic nib of the X4 pen.

Typical for pen displays - slight texture. Pen didn't feel slippery on the glass.

Similar feel to Wacom Movink 13 - just very slightly less

### Connections and cabling

* There are two USB-C ports. The included full-featured USB-C cable only fits in the top port. The bottom port is for the 3-in-1 cable.
* With the full-featured USB-C cable plugged into the top port, the cable crowds the brightness button, which makes it a little awkward to press. Not a big deal.
* With just the full-featured USB-C cable, it worked with my M3 MacBook Pro and my Surface Laptop 7.
* The full-featured cable is about 1.5 to 2 m. It's thick and sturdy - not as easy to handle as some of the really thin cables.
* It's great that the full-featured USB-C cable is included. A cable like this costs something like $20 or $30, and lots of tablets don't include it. You also get the 3-in-1 cable, so you won't be missing any cables.

### Wireless

NO. The tablet requires a cable.

### Turning off the screen

On my Mac, the power button turns off the entire tablet, including the pen. The driver then says no tablet is attached. So you can't use the power button to turn off the screen and use the tablet as a screenless pen tablet. I didn't get to test this on Windows.

## Other inputs

### Buttons

* 8 buttons on the left side
* They can be pressed from the left and from the top
* It is NOT hard to press the buttons when the tablet is resting on a desk
* The red dots on the buttons are just paint. They don't light up, and there are no bumps you can feel, so you'll have to know where the buttons are by touch or look at them.
* The buttons are not flush with the tablet. They stick out by about a millimeter. Because they can be pressed from the side, be careful how you pack the tablet - something might press them.

### Rollers

* 2 rollers on the left side
* The rollers provide tactile feedback as you roll them and have a "click" sound
* The rollers were could easily be used even when the tablet is resting on a desk
* The click is not loud, but it's medium level. If you're using the tablet around people, like in a coffee shop, they will hear it. That's not a complaint - it's just probably going to be surprising that it has a click.
* By default the top roller changes the brush size.
* If you pick up the tablet by the left edge, you can accidentally roll a roller by a step. Not a big deal, because you can disable them.
* A little bit of plastic extends out from the edge around the rollers, so the rollers are a bit protected.

### Touch

NO

At 100% brightness for an hour - seems overall room temperature with some light heat increasing toward the ports on the right

## Driver

* macOS: the XP-Pen driver I already had installed (version 4.0.10) recognized the tablet and the X4 pen.
* When I connected it, macOS asked me to allow the accessory to connect. Then the Keyboard Setup Assistant came up, because computers often think tablets with buttons are keyboards. You can just quit it.

## Usage with Android

I tested with a Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra.

* The phone can't power the tablet over USB-C by itself.
* Once I gave the tablet external power through the 3-in-1 cable (only the power end connected, to a USB power supply), Samsung DeX came up on the display.
* Pressure did not work in Concepts or in Infinite Painter. Pressure did work on a web-based pressure tester in Chrome, so it's at least theoretically possible.
* Navigating DeX is awkward because the display has no touch.
* It drains the phone's battery a lot.
* Overall it's a mixed bag.
