# XP-Pen Magic Pro 13 (MDP1311) notes

## Overview

OVERALL RATING: EXCELLENT

As of November 2026, this is the most versatile standalone drawing tablet available. Its pure drawing experience is highly comparable to the [Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 (DTH-A140)](../wacom/wacom-movinkpad/wacom-dtha140-notes.md), while adding native hardware **DP-IN** pen display support and **DP-OUT** external display mirroring—making it a strong contender for my standalone drawing tablet of the year in 2026.

### Why this tablet is important

When Wacom released the MovinkPad Pro 14, it finally gave us a standalone Android tablet with a truly great drawing experience—but it lacked native hardware DP-IN and DP-OUT support.

The XP-Pen Magic Pro 13 delivers what I have always wanted from this category: one device that genuinely covers **three usage modes** without hacky software workarounds:

* A standalone Android drawing tablet
* A native hardware **DP-IN** pen display for Mac and Windows (at full native resolution and 120Hz)
* **DP-OUT** mirroring to an external monitor

Wacom and other manufacturers should take note—this is how standalone drawing tablets are supposed to work.

### How to think about this tablet

Think of this as a true hybrid device: both a standalone Android drawing tablet and a 13-inch ultra-high-resolution, 120Hz pen display.

If you *only* need a desk pen display, a dedicated pen display is normally less expensive. However, almost all 13-inch pen displays are 1920×1080 (with a few at 2560×1440) and 60Hz. If you want a super-high-resolution (~3K class), **120Hz**, **3:2** 13-inch pen display—or want to draw at your desk connected to a computer and also take the tablet away as a standalone device—this is currently in a class of its own.

### Price/Performance

While not cheap, you get substantially more for your money here than with the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 (~$1,100 USD / ~$1,500 CAD). I bought mine on discount for $900 USD, and creator discount codes (such as one shared by Teoh on Tech) brought the price down to $750 USD. Given everything it does, this is a great value.

### What would make this tablet even better

I hope future updates or generations address a few things:

* Report the tablet's battery percentage inside the XP-Pen desktop driver (or via an indicator) when running in hardware DP-IN pen display mode, and expose a battery usage history graph in Android settings
* Enough power delivery over a single laptop USB-C connection so the battery does not slowly trickle down during long pen display sessions
* Support for X3 Pro Pen specialty features (such as the tail eraser) in the driver
* Slightly grippier rubber feet on the back (closer to the MovinkPad Pro 14) and no rear camera glass resting near the desk
* Tap-to-wake support enabled out of the box
* Smoother file handoff/syncing when moving an active drawing between desktop pen display mode and standalone Android mode

## Notes on specs

### Device

* Model number: `MDP1311`
* Color: White (true white, not off-white)
* Weight: TBD *(not weighed on my scale yet; in hand it felt similar or very slightly heavier than the MovinkPad Pro 14, and heavier than a 13-inch iPad Pro)*
* Size and aspect ratio:
  * 13-inch screen with a **3:2** aspect ratio (noticeably more square than the 16:10 Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14—3:2 is one of my favorite aspect ratios)
  * Physically slightly shorter and narrower than the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14
  * Slightly shorter, wider, and heavier than a 13-inch Apple iPad Pro

### Standalone

* CPU: MediaTek Dimensity 8300
* RAM: TBD *(not listed on the retail box)*
* Storage: 256 GB
* Cameras: YES (has a rear camera)

### Display

* Resolution: ~3K class (`3000×2000` class 3:2 panel—pixel density is high enough that at native resolution on macOS, the menu bar is tiny until UI scaling is enabled)
* Refresh rate: **120Hz** (confirmed 120Hz in macOS display settings)
* Color gamut (box spec): 100% sRGB, 96% DCI-P3

### What's in the box

* XP-Pen Magic Pro 13 tablet (`MDP1311`)
* White XP-Pen X4 Pen (`PD52`)
* Replacement nibs and nib extractor
* Tray / reset pin tool
* Drawing glove and user manual
* White USB-C video/data cable

### Pens

* Included pen: **[XP-Pen X4 Pen](../../pens/xppen-pens/xppen-x4pen-notes.md) (`PD52`)** — white finish, two configurable side buttons, no tail eraser (16,000 pressure levels advertised on box)

#### Compatible Pens

* **[XP-Pen X3 Pro Pen](../../pens/xppen-pens/xppen-x3propen-notes.md)** — WORKS for drawing, pressure, and the two configurable side buttons.
  * However, the driver does not distinguish it from the X4 Pen, and **X3 Pro–specific features such as the tail eraser do NOT work** (and specialty controls like the X3 Pro Roller wheel likely will not work either).

#### Incompatible Pens

* **[XP-Pen X3 Elite Pen](../../pens/xppen-pens/xppen-x3elitepen-notes.md)** — Does **NOT** work.
* Wacom EMR pens (such as Pro Pen 3) — Not compatible.

## Standalone experience

### Performance

Very responsive in my testing with Infinite Painter and Clip Studio Paint, matching the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 in side-by-side responsiveness.

### First run

Initial Android setup was quick—faster than setting up Windows. I signed in via a passkey QR code on my phone and declined device backup and Google Gemini assistant prompts.

It boots into a clean, elegant sunrise-style default wallpaper—subtle and much nicer than loud graphics, and a bit more visually interesting than the MovinkPad Pro 14's plain gray background. *(Note: I did accidentally trigger a pre-installed/promoted Temu icon once while navigating the home screen.)*

### App compatibility

It has full support for the Google Play Store.

Apps I installed or tested without issue:

* **Infinite Painter** — installed from Play Store; ran smoothly with low brush lag
* **Clip Studio Paint** — pre-installed (launches into Doodle/Simple Mode tutorial on first run, which was annoying to click through before switching to full Studio Mode)
* **ibisPaint X** — installed from Play Store

## Device experience

## Build quality

EXCELLENT. Even the packaging looks great, and the tablet itself has a clean, attractive all-white design that feels solid and premium. *(One viewer raised a fair question about whether the white plastic finish might stain over time—long-term wear is TBD.)*

### Size and drawing on the go

At 13 inches with a 3:2 aspect ratio, it is a terrific size on a desk—both as a standalone tablet and as a pen display. Like the 14-inch MovinkPad Pro, it is still a little large for casual handheld use or drawing in bed, where I personally prefer an 11-inch tablet or iPad mini.

### Camera

Unlike the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14, this tablet includes a rear camera. While it sat fine on my desk, I am generally less fond of having camera glass facing the desk surface compared to Wacom's completely flat, camera-free back.

### Tap to wake

I tested tapping the sleeping screen with both my finger and the pen—**tap-to-wake did not turn the screen on** (at least by default). You have to press the physical power button on the top-left edge.

## Display experience

### Pixel sharpness and colors

* Pixels are very sharp thanks to the ~3K resolution on a 13-inch panel.
* Default colors look vibrant, elegant, and slightly saturated out of the box.

### Refresh rate

Runs at **120Hz**, which I also verified directly in macOS display settings when connected in pen display mode. You get both high resolution and 120Hz—something very rare in 13-inch pen displays.

### PWM flicker

NONE OBSERVED. At a viewer's request, I tested the screen using my phone camera in Pro mode at **1/12,000s** shutter speed (with increased ISO) while sweeping screen brightness from 0% to 100%. I did not observe any PWM flickering or black bars at any brightness setting.

## Drawing performance

OVERALL: EXCELLENT — very close to the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14.

### Customizing tablet behavior

* **In standalone Android mode:** Like other Android standalone tablets, pressure curves are configured inside individual drawing apps (such as Clip Studio Paint or Infinite Painter).
* **In DP-IN pen display mode:** Uses the desktop **XP-Pen driver** (tested on macOS with driver **v4.0.2**, released October 29). It exposes standard XP-Pen options including work-area/monitor mapping, pressure curve adjustment, and configuring the two pen side buttons.
  * *macOS driver mapping quirk:* Right after installing the driver, my pen cursor moved off the tablet onto my MacBook screen even though the driver already showed the correct display mapped. Toggling the target monitor to the other display and switching it back immediately fixed the mapping.

### Tilt

EXCELLENT.

* Tilt was broken on XP-Pen's earlier Magic Note Pad and Magic Drawing Pad (where strokes would jump erratically even during smooth motion).
* On the Magic Pro 13, **tilt works smoothly and predictably** with no jumping or weird behavior.

### Pressure and IAF (Initial Activation Force)

VERY GOOD.

* Pressure response felt natural and smooth across light sketching, shading, and cross-hatching.
* I compared the **X4 Pen** directly against the **Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 (Pro Pen 3)** using an exaggerated high-sensitivity pressure curve. The **Wacom Pro Pen 3 has a very slightly lower IAF**—you have to press marginally harder to activate the X4 Pen at ultra-light pressure—but the gap is small enough that most artists will never notice it unless they are accustomed to extremely light activation force.

### Surface texture

VERY GOOD.

* Since surface feel depends on both the glass and the pen nib, I cross-tested both pens across both tablets.
* Comparing each tablet with its own stock pen, the Magic Pro 13 + X4 Pen feels **very close—just very slightly less textured**—than the MovinkPad Pro 14 + Pro Pen 3. It has pleasant control and is not overly slippery.

### Pointer lag

NORMAL for a standalone Android drawing tablet (on par with the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14).

* Side-by-side testing in **Clip Studio Paint** (G-Pen with stabilization turned off; toggling Stroke Preview in preferences made no noticeable difference) showed **essentially identical pointer lag** between the Magic Pro 13 and the MovinkPad Pro 14.
* **Infinite Painter** showed noticeably less brush lag than Clip Studio Paint on both tablets, likely due to stroke position prediction in Infinite Painter.

### Using the tablet as a pen display

EXCELLENT. Unlike the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 (which lacks hardware DP-IN and relies on software-based [Wacom Instant Pen Display](../../apps/wacom-instant-pen-display.md)) or older Android tablets that rely on laggy camera-viewfinder tricks, the Magic Pro 13 switches cleanly at the hardware level.

As soon as you plug a compatible USB-C cable into the **upper USB-C port**, the Android UI blanks out and the tablet enters **"Drawing Display Mode"** automatically with no permission dialogs on the tablet. Testing in **Krita** on an **M3 MacBook Pro**, it behaved just like a dedicated external pen display at full native resolution and **120Hz**, with **no extra latency** beyond normal pen display behavior.

## Connections and cabling

### Ports

The tablet has **two USB-C ports**, and **they are NOT interchangeable**:

* **Upper USB-C port:** **DP-IN** — connects to a Mac or PC to use the tablet as a pen display.
* **Middle USB-C port:** **DP-OUT** — connects to an external monitor for display mirroring (and accepts charging power).

*(If you plug your computer into the middle port by mistake, pen display mode will not trigger—make sure to use the upper port for DP-IN and the middle port for DP-OUT.)*

### DP-IN support and battery behavior

* **Native USB-C (DP Alt Mode / Thunderbolt):** **YES.** Worked plug-and-play with an M3 MacBook Pro using the included white XP-Pen USB-C cable (and also with a Thunderbolt cable) plugged into the upper port.
* **Battery drain while connected as a pen display:**
  * While in hardware DP-IN mode, you cannot see the Android status bar, the XP-Pen desktop driver does not display battery percentage, and Android's battery settings did not show a historical usage graph.
  * After running connected solely to my M3 MacBook Pro over USB-C during testing, the tablet's battery dropped by **2%** (from 49% to 47%), indicating a laptop USB-C port alone does not supply enough power to keep it fully topped up indefinitely. Multi-hour drain behavior is TBD.

### Connecting DP-IN to computers without USB-C video (Adapter & 3-in-1 Cable Tests)

Because many desktops and older laptops only have HDMI or DisplayPort rather than USB-C video out, I tested several conversion setups into the **upper USB-C port**:

* **Bidirectional USB-C to DisplayPort + USB-A cable (tested on a Windows mini PC):** **WORKED.** Both video and pen display mode worked over DisplayPort.
* **USB-C to HDMI + USB-A splitter cables and USB-C hub HDMI adapters (tested on both Mac and Windows mini PC):** **DID NOT WORK for video.** Touch/pen input reached the computer (and Windows detected an external screen stuck at 640×480), but the tablet screen stayed dark—likely because most HDMI-to-USB-C cables are unidirectional in the wrong direction or run into HDMI conversion quirks.
* **XP-Pen 3-in-1 cable (from a recent XP-Pen Artist 12 Pro, tested both unpowered and with external power):** **DID NOT WORK.** The PC did not recognize display or touch/pen input at all. Do not assume 3-in-1 cables from other XP-Pen tablets will work with the Magic Pro 13.

### DP-OUT support (External Monitor Mirroring)

* **Supported (Mirroring only):** **YES.** Connecting the **middle USB-C port** to an external monitor prompts **"Mirror to external display"** at the bottom of the tablet screen. It mirrors the tablet display only (no extended desktop mode).
* **Latency when mirrored:** **NONE OBSERVED.** Drawing strokes appeared completely in sync between the tablet and the external monitor.
* **Cable and power requirements:**
  * Cable choice was finicky: third-party Thunderbolt cables failed to establish a signal in several tries, whereas the **included white XP-Pen USB-C cable** worked reliably with a generic HD monitor (I could not get DP-OUT video onto a Wacom Movink 13).
  * **External monitor needs its own power:** Even when I plugged external wall power into the Magic Pro 13's upper USB-C port, the tablet could **not** deliver enough power over the white cable to power an external monitor by itself. Once I powered the external monitor directly, it worked—and the monitor even fed charging power back into the Magic Pro 13.

## Other inputs

### Touch

Touch input worked smoothly on Android and also passed through to Windows during USB-C connection tests.

## Ergonomics

### Staying in place on a desk

There are **four rubber feet** on the back. It is not slippery in normal drawing use, though when I pushed both tablets sideways on my desk back-to-back, the Magic Pro 13 slid with noticeably less force than the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14, whose rubber feet grip more firmly.

## Choosing between tablets

### Versus Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 (DTH-A140)

In pure standalone drawing feel—surface texture, responsiveness, and brush lag in Clip Studio Paint and Infinite Painter—these two tablets are remarkably close.

**pick the XP-Pen Magic Pro 13 if:**

* you want **one device for three roles**: standalone Android drawing tablet, native **120Hz ~3K hardware DP-IN pen display**, and **DP-OUT** external display mirroring
* you want significantly more versatility and better value for your money (~$750–$900 USD vs. ~$1,100 USD / ~$1,500 CAD)
* you prefer a **3:2** screen aspect ratio at 13 inches
* you already use XP-Pen X3 Pro pens as backups (basic drawing and two side buttons work, minus the eraser)

**pick the Wacom MovinkPad Pro 14 if:**

* you want the lowest possible Initial Activation Force (**Wacom Pro Pen 3**) and very slightly grippier etched-glass texture
* you want a slightly larger **14-inch 16:10 OLED** panel with no rear camera and firmer desk grip
* you only need a standalone Android drawing tablet (or are fine with software-based Wacom Instant Pen Display) and prefer the Wacom ecosystem

### Versus Apple iPad + Apple Pencil Pro

Physically, a 13-inch iPad Pro is slightly taller, slightly narrower, and noticeably lighter than the Magic Pro 13, though users who like the squarish footprint of the 13-inch iPad Pro will feel right at home with the Magic Pro 13's 3:2 shape.
